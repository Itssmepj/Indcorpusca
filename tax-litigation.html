Tax Litigation FAQ

Answers to Common Tax Dispute Questions

Do not ignore it. Section 148 relates to reopening of assessment where income has allegedly escaped assessment. First, verify the mandatory Document Identification Number (DIN). Second, verify whether statutory pre-inquiry under Section 148A was conducted. Third, file the return of income under protest within the stipulated timeframe and formally request the "reasons to believe" and sanction approvals to challenge jurisdictional validity.

Filing an appeal does not automatically stay tax recovery. You must file a formal Stay Petition under Section 220(6) before the Assessing Officer and Commissioner. Under CBDT circulars, depositing 20% of the disputed demand typically stays recovery of the remaining 80% pending disposal by the CIT(A). In genuine hardship or prima facie illegal assessments, higher forums can grant complete stay without pre-deposit.

An appeal before the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) must be filed electronically in Form 35 within 30 days from the date of service of the assessment order. An appeal before the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) must be filed in Form 36 within 60 days of receiving the CIT(A) order. In GST matters, appeal before the Appellate Authority must be filed in Form GST APL-01 within 3 months.

Under Faceless Assessment (NeAC), all submissions, evidence, and written rebuttals are submitted digitally through the IT e-filing portal. If an adverse variation to your returned income is proposed in a Show Cause Notice, you are entitled to request an oral hearing conducted via secure video conferencing. Our litigation team prepares the digital paper-book and argues your case during these scheduled video hearings.

Under binding CBDT circulars and affirmed by the Supreme Court of India, any notice, order, or statutory communication issued by the Income Tax Department without a system-generated Document Identification Number (DIN) is treated as invalid and void ab initio (having never existed in law), providing a strong preliminary ground for quashing the proceeding.
